### Manual artwork: initial-release alternative

No uploads, preview URLs or automatic customer approvals have been enabled. Emailed attachments remain untrusted. The owner must demonstrate a workable, private, patched file-review/archive process; free edge decoding and pixel-bomb resistance have not been proven. Do not open unknown attachments in the admin browser or fetch arbitrary image URLs on the server.

1. Owner accepts the order and confirms design instructions. Receive JPG/PNG artwork by the agreed email route, referencing the order. Store it privately outside GitHub. Apply the owner's reviewed malware/file-type/size/pixel checks and make a safe proof export with updated local software. If safe handling cannot be demonstrated, physical fulfilment remains blocked.
2. For every distinct ordered design, produce an identified final proof showing both sides together, or the expressly agreed single side, and record its SHA-256 and an opaque private archive reference. Up to five design entries must all appear in the registered proof version. Keep the corresponding front/back originals clearly matched. Respect the receiving mailbox's actual attachment limits by splitting labelled messages if necessary; do not promise that a 100 MB order fits one email. The digest identifies exactly which proof the customer reviewed; it does not itself certify image safety. Never invent a hash of a nonexistent proof.
3. Use **Register reviewed emailed proof version**. Confirm safe owner review and record the private delivery/archive reference. Send that exact version and all design labels to the customer, asking for an explicit written approval. Do not print on a viewing/open event or silence.
4. Match the actual reply to the order's customer email through the reviewed human verification process, identify the approved version/designs and archive the real reply privately. Use **Record actual customer proof approval**, entering its digest, evidence reference, sender and actual received timestamp. The owner attests to what they independently checked; this is not automated email-authenticity verification.
5. A new proof version resets approval. Old replies cannot approve a later version. Approval is immutable; proof changes after production starts are blocked and require owner intervention rather than rewriting history. Only the owner with current MFA may register/attest; staff cannot self-approve.
6. Independently verify the required received funds, including shipping, before authorising print production. SQL still enforces owner acceptance, payment, latest proof approval and item transitions. Service work may start after the deposit in its accepted quote, but reaching ready/completed still requires the full amount. A deposit is never represented as fully paid.
7. For external refunds, first record owner approval, then independently confirm the bank transfer and use **Record independently verified external refund**. This records evidence; it does not move money. Refund records are immutable/idempotent and reports show confirmed refunds separately. A refund does not automatically amend agreed scope/prices; review cancellation/adjustments and any remaining recorded balance before requesting further payment. Production is held for refund review.
8. Maintain the private attachment/proof/reply inventory. Retention preparation also includes manual-proof orders. Review completion+83-day notices, lawful holds and completion+90-day deletion with at least seven days' delivered notice. Use the owner-only hold action and keep its private evidence reference. After independently deleting the temporary owner-held images/proofs under the reviewed notice/hold checks, use **Record completed external artwork deletion** with the actual deletion-log reference. The database refuses early/held recording; the result is explicitly an owner attestation, not a claim that the app deleted external bytes. Preserve required approval/financial evidence. The app cannot automatically delete email attachments, off-site copies or backups. Until this real procedure and backup retention are rehearsed, manual artwork operations are not launch-verified. At 100 MB/order, 100 orders/month and 90-day retention can require roughly 30 GB before backups: an existing private archive with adequate capacity is required, not an assumed free mailbox allowance.

## Backups: practical free preparation

Git tracks code/migrations, **not customer data or private artwork**. Use encrypted local/off-site files on an owner-controlled device and existing off-site storage with adequate free space; no unauthorised cloud account or paid backup service was created. Keep the encryption key separately, preferably in an existing password manager, and test recovery access.

For a future approved hosted staging drill:

1. Freeze writes and outbox sending, record migration/version/time and capture row counts and financial sums. Inventory private object keys, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes without publishing paths.
2. Follow Supabase's current export procedure to produce protected schema/data/role exports, including the private `netbud` schema; review Auth-managed schema limitations. Use a local credential store or temporary protected pgpass file, never connection passwords in shared command history. A generic `pg_dump --schema=public` would omit NetBud's data.
3. Download actual private bucket bytes separately through an authorised server tool. Database backups do not include those bytes. Encrypt the database exports, object bytes and manifest before moving them off-site. Verify copied hashes. Do not use GitHub artifacts or the public repository as a backup destination.
4. Restore into a separate disposable staging project following provider restore instructions. Restore reviewed roles/grants/schema/data and private objects, then reconcile financial sums, accepted quotes, order references, file counts/hashes and RLS. Do not overwrite a live project.
5. Revoke restored guest sessions, pending OTPs and staff sessions; rotate relevant secrets. Keep restored outbox sending off and quarantine all queued/uncertain events to avoid replaying old messages. Re-establish staff Auth/MFA safely; never assume database rows restore provider sessions or MFA factors.
6. Test owner login/MFA, cross-customer denial, quote/manual payment totals and private files. Record measured recovery time, recoverable timestamp and any gaps. Resume only after owner review; destroy the disposable copy under the approved retention plan.

Hosted PostgreSQL tools, provider credentials, off-site destination/permissions, an always-on free scheduler and the full hosted restore drill remain unverified. Local machines sleeping/offline prevent dependable automation; no zero-cost unattended recovery guarantee is offered. A manual daily export before/after material business changes is a proposed target, not an implemented SLA.

`backupFixture` / `restoreFixture` demonstrate an authenticated encrypted PGlite archive plus private-byte manifest. Tests restore into a **fresh** engine, verify records and bytes and reject wrong keys/tampering. ## Failure response

- Backend unavailable/cap reached: preserve input, fail safely, retry orders with the same idempotency key; never claim payment or mail delivery.
- Mail failed/uncertain: owner checks provider dashboard and message ID before any resend. Do not automatically replay a timeout or an event outside the idempotency window. Suppress bounced/complained recipients and investigate before future sends; an account-level suppression reconciliation drill remains required.
- Storage near 80% of the 900 MB local budget: review retained/quarantined/reserved/orphan data and future deletion notices. At capacity, deny admission. Compare DB bookkeeping with actual provider Storage usage before allowing uploads.
- DB nearing 400 MB: stop new write-heavy intake and investigate growth; don't automatically upgrade. Existing guard cannot perfectly measure concurrent provider overhead.
- Suspected compromise: disable new routes/sending, revoke sessions/keys, preserve minimal audit evidence, notify owner and follow reviewed incident/privacy procedures.

No cron, real outgoing message, image upload, customer order or payment was performed for this runbook.
